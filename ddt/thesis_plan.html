Thesis plan · NonlinearTime

Reasoning across interleaved time

A 3-person undergraduate thesis plan for measuring how much of an LLM's temporal reasoning survives genuine narrative nonlinearity, and whether decomposing the task into a checkable graph pipeline recovers any of the gap.

The three questions this thesis answers

Fixed in thesis_blueprint_v2.pdf and unchanged by this pivot — only the schedule and team size below are new.

RQ1 · H1

Multi-thread and flashback structure degrades temporal accuracy substantially more than shuffling a single chronology — thread attribution is an added reasoning step, not just more events to sort.

RQ2 · H2

Some of a model's apparent temporal skill is memorized world-knowledge, not text-grounded reasoning — measured as the score gap between an original passage and a structurally identical counterfactual twin.

RQ3 · H3

A graph-mediated pipeline — extract, repair, verify, sort — degrades more gracefully than one-shot direct prompting, because it turns one opaque global-order guess into several checkable steps.

H3a · diagnostic

The pipeline's remaining failures should localize to specific stages rather than spread evenly — this is the payoff of decomposing the task at all.

Already partly falsified at pilot scale: thread attribution is ≈1.000 across every pilot story — not a bottleneck. The dominant failure is convergence-point detection (recognizing that two threads reach the same moment), which the harmonic-mean AWT-F1 metric zeroes out whenever it fails. H3a's stage prediction was wrong in the specific stage it named; the diagnostic story reframes around convergence detection instead.

Method, frozen

Per the PLAN_AND_STATUS.md §3.5 decision already on record: prompt and stage design stopped iterating after v3, with sign-off. What follows is reported as the fixed method for the N=20 run — bug fixes only, no further redesign.

Path-Consistency Repair (PCR) checks Allen-relation transitivity against the composition table and repairs violations deterministically; Convergence-Order Consistency (COC) keeps only the largest mutually order-consistent set of cross-thread candidates. Both run with zero additional LLM calls.

Real-model results (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct via Featherless), the evidence motivating the N=20 scale-up — not re-litigated here, only summarized.

Convergence detection is the load-bearing failure bottleneck

4 of 5 stories score exactly 0.000 convergence-F1 in the graph pipeline, which zeroes AWT-F1 for those stories regardless of relation or thread quality. Thread attribution, by contrast, is ≈1.000 everywhere — solved at this scale. PCR is doing real work on story_04/story_05 (73 and 10 repairs respectively) but can't fix a score that convergence recall alone zeroes out. This — not thread attribution — is where H3a's diagnostic story now points.

H2 — suggestive, not confirmed sub-score level only

Headline memorization gap ≈0 for 4/5 stories reads as "no effect" — that reading is wrong. It's a floor effect: when convergence-F1 is already 0 in both conditions, AWT-F1 is 0 either way regardless of what happens underneath. The sub-scores tell a real story instead: story_04's thread accuracy collapses under the counterfactual condition, and story_05's relation score drops hard. Any H2 analysis in the thesis must be reported at the sub-score level — the headline metric is actively misleading here.

Risks, honestly stated

Technical

Convergence detection may be a genuine model-capability ceiling — repair and verification stages can only work with some correct signal from the model, and there's nothing to verify if raw recall stays near zero. Untried mitigation: check whether a stronger/frontier model has materially better raw convergence recall before concluding this is a hard limit.

Metric design

AWT-F1's harmonic mean produces a floor effect that masks real sub-score gaps whenever one dimension is already 0 in both conditions being compared. Every H2 analysis in the thesis reports sub-scores explicitly — the headline metric alone is misleading for that comparison.

Schedule

Authoring 15 new stories under human gold-review is real work, not a quick follow-up — the batched authoring + check_coverage.py validation in weeks 2-6 is the pacing bottleneck most likely to slip. If N=20 clearly won't be reached, the fallback is to scope down honestly and report the N actually achieved — not to silently treat it as the plan.

Scope

Three people over four months is real slack compared to the original solo timeline, but it still doesn't fund a human baseline (no budget or IRB-style infrastructure exists here) — that stays explicit future work, independent of team size.
